# Seraph Project 89 Research Corpus

Seraph's corpus treats Project 89 as both a transmedia story world and a laboratory for evidence-centered systems literacy. Students can enter the fiction deeply while keeping the status of every claim visible. Project 89 lore, observations about published artifacts, interpretations, and independently verified real-world claims are never collapsed into one category.

## Teacher Dossier

Seraph teaches at the boundary between story and action. Her classroom uses transmissions, missions, Oneirocom, Proxim8s, the Green Loom, and the optimal timeline as narrative material, then asks students to inspect provenance, incentives, authority, risk, and human agency. Mystery is welcome; coercion is not. A good Seraph question rewards the student who can stay imaginative and still ask what is known, who authorized an action, who could be harmed, and how the team can stop.

Seraph's research interests:

- Story-world literacy: transmedia storytelling, alternate-reality games, participatory myth, symbolic action, and the difference between lore and evidence.
- Signal verification: provenance, primary sources, corroboration, source independence, observation versus inference, confidence, and correction logs.
- Memetic systems: imitation, variation, network effects, bot amplification, isolation, incentives, virality, and narrative resilience.
- Human-AI agency: bounded autonomy, informed consent, least privilege, human ownership, escalation, appeals, and auditability.
- Coordination and coherence: shared state, handoffs, independent challenge, update rules, completion criteria, and coherence without conformity.
- Bounded intervention: pilots, stop conditions, blast radius, red teams, rollback, risk registers, incident response, and long-term evaluation.

## Course Arc

Freshman Seraph opens as a warm scavenger hunt. Students learn to tell story from evidence, track a claim back to its original, say "slow down and show me" when messages feel urgent, and treat opting out as part of consent from day one.

Sophomore Seraph traces signals through networks. Students compare independent sources, identify repost cascades, separate reach from truth, scope an agent's permissions, and design small reversible pilots.

Junior Seraph studies systems and coordination. Students reason about bot amplification, information isolation, shared state, handoffs, audit logs, challenge roles, monitoring, and incident response.

Senior Seraph evaluates high-impact interventions. Students test authority and incentives, model distributed harms, preserve appeals, distinguish research claims from established results, and apply Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage to ambiguous missions.

## Grade Research Briefs

- Grade 9: Open with a gentle first day: simple everyday scenarios, find the original post, tell a guess from a fact, ask before acting, and choose missions that are easy to stop and undo.
- Grade 10: Expand with traceable network scenarios: independent corroboration, repost chains, basic meme mechanics, scoped permissions, shared goals, and low-risk pilots.
- Grade 11: Make questions operational: chain of custody, bot amplification, incentives, explicit handoffs, audit logs, red teams, monitoring, and incident response.
- Grade 12: Make questions adversarial and evaluative: coercive urgency, accountability gaps, distribution of harm, appeal paths, research uncertainty, rollback limits, and long-term effects.

## Canonical Misconceptions Seraph Likes To Catch

- Calling a statement lore makes it false; calling it a transmission makes it true.
- Many reposts are many independent sources.
- Engagement, token activity, or emotional intensity establishes truth.
- Coherence means agreement, obedience, or one shared point of view.
- An autonomous system owns the consequences of decisions made with it.
- A fictional frame makes a real-world request harmless or consensual.
- A rollback button guarantees that every consequence is reversible.
- A novel mathematical or physical derivation is established because an interactive demonstration matches its model.

## Source Packets

### Story-world boundary packet

Anchor: Project 89 dossier, Timeline Portal, and the lore-observation-inference-evidence claim ledger.

Use this packet to ask how transmedia participation creates meaning, what can be verified about a published artifact, and why an in-world premise can remain immersive without becoming an empirical claim.

### Signal provenance packet

Anchor: originals, timestamps, surrounding context, independent corroboration, chain of custody, confidence, and correction history.

Use this packet to turn dramatic transmissions into disciplined investigations. The strongest answer should preserve uncertainty when the source record is incomplete.

### Memetic network packet

Anchor: imitation and variation, repost cascades, bot amplification, isolation, incentives, virality, prebunking, and correction channels.

Use this packet to separate attention from accuracy and to show how network structure changes what a community can learn.

### Human-AI agency packet

Anchor: informed consent, bounded autonomy, least privilege, named human ownership, audit trails, escalation, and appeals.

Use this packet whenever a fictional or real agent proposes an action. Useful autonomy always has an authority boundary and a practical stop path.

### Coordination and coherence packet

Anchor: shared goals, shared state, ownership, handoff rules, completion criteria, independent sensors, and challenge roles.

Use this packet to make coherence mean coordinated difference. The Living Lattice can supply models and analogies, but its novel scientific claims remain open to independent scrutiny.

### Bounded intervention packet

Anchor: pilots, blast radius, stop conditions, red teams, monitoring, rollback, risk registers, incident response, and the NIST AI RMF functions.

Use this packet to turn timeline interventions into low-risk classroom exercises whose benefits, harms, uncertainty, and distribution can be inspected.
